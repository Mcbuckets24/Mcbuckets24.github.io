3. One night, when I was a child, my brother and I played the Black Ops Zombies map Origins while my parents weren’t home. For whatever reason, this game provided us with the perfect medium to connect. Shared memories, shared joys, and a moment to live our lives in parallel. The game has played a vast role as an anchor and pillar of my personal story. I spent many afternoons after school trying to avoid my head diving into a game I was incredibly passionate about. Call of Duty Zombies has never faded from my life. There’s an old friend, someone who I haven’t talked to in almost 6 years who I was bonded to through Call of Duty Zombies. It was the first time I had connected with someone over a shared hobby, and it became a little light that I learned to hold onto. No matter how much the world shakes, how I or my environment changes, Call of Duty Zombies has been the same since the day it launched. A journey I still take pride in, and a shared experience with so many people over the years that still holds a special space in my mind.↑

6. Some point around the time I saw this video essay, I learned that to express myself, I needed to learn how to write. It’s a small video about hiking the Appalachian Trail and the journeys within that often spawns. It made me think about the world a little differently, getting “lost in the silly forests within.” This video helped me find my voice. The one I write this very essay with now. The way we adopt the language of those around us. How we learn to speak not by burying our heads in a dictionary but getting out of the world and hearing what everyone else has to say. Then listening, evaluating, and then recontextualizing your own world after learning of theirs. I always come back to this. It’s the heart of my spirit. When I begin to tumble, to lose my way, to fall, I come back here. I let its words consume me. I let its message wash over me, and I feel home. Home in a story that taught me the value of now. The value of living through the struggle. Through the tumult just to see the new stories, new friends, and new memories awaiting us on the other side. So maybe the answer isn’t a hut in the woods, I’m sure for all of us, it once felt like it. But I think that failing, learning, living, and loving, here with those we care about is the best. Despite how hard it may feel. I think our lives are meant to be shared. To be understood not through our deep inner ponderings, but by the small remark of a stranger who seems to boil your entire life down to a couple of simple words that open your eyes to everything you’ve been missing. That’s what this video was for me. “I’d even gently suggest that perhaps the strangest adventure. Perhaps the most humbling one happens on the inside. Discovering those parts of yourself you’ve never acknowledged, jettisoning those parts you’ve never liked and constantly, constantly. Being aware that you will soon turn into someone else and who that is, is decided at every moment by you now and in two minutes and two minutes after that until 10 years later, one very long succession of two minutes. You’ll find a new you sitting there, and you’re not even sure where they began, nor where they’ll end, which makes us custodians of a sort looking after this body until a new, hopefully better us inhabits it a while from now.”↑

8. This video holds a double value. One of the chronologically earlier entries on the list, both Jschlatt and Minecraft hold a wealth of significance in my story. Minecraft, as it was for most people in my generation’s childhood, was extremely important. It was one of the first games I ever really learned to play, the game that taught me that the fun of life comes from sharing it with others. Some of my old school friends and I still have a Minecraft world that we’ve been playing on for over 7 years now. Some of my favorite memories were made in that world. Thousands of hours in one another’s company, and something I’m sure we’ll remember forever. What was, looking back, an important creative outlet for me, and the beginnings of my art centered mind. But what about the other half of this entry’s meaning? The actual entry is a YouTube video by a creator named Jschlatt. In it, he talks about everything I just mentioned, and the beauty of Minecraft. But what interests me more, is Jschlatt himself. An almost mysterious figure over the years. Someone that’s done everything from the worst jokes to some of the most personal content I’ve ever interacted with. I think Jschlatt almost served as an unknowing mentor figure for me. I sort of idolized his ability to view his own life. The quiet musings he would give in his commentary videos that would make me rethink my own circumstances in ways I’d never thought of before. The diverse interest set he had that I slowly found aligned with my own. The niche games, the old technology, the camcorders, all of it. Growing up in the age of the internet, so many people spoke of the dangers of parasocial relationships. This idea that someone behind the screen was an actual friend. I never got to that stage, but for me to sit here and write that Jschlatt wasn’t an important part of my self-discovery would be a lie. I’m not sure what that says about growing up on the internet, but it’s something I continue to reflect on.↑

Somewhere in my heart, I still think about this game. And it still keeps me warm when I need it. Breath of the Wild, to me, despite how large it was, had nothing to say. That was its greatest genius. 12. Not many people can say that something in their life has stayed the same for 7 years. One Piece is no different from the day I started reading it back in middle school. Life changes, the world changes, people change, but One Piece has been released almost weekly for almost 29 years. Through deaths, pandemics, failures, all of the above, One Piece has never changed. It still comes out every week and still holds itself to the high standard it set the day I started reading it. A silly story about pirates trying to find a treasure that’s been running for almost 30 years. The community has lifted me up when I needed it, the story has unknowingly saved me when I needed a light, One Piece has always been there through it all. It’s covered everything in its 1172 chapters at the time of writing. For every moment of discomfort or stumble I’ve encountered, somewhere within its text I’ve found some sort of solace. One Piece means everything to me not only for how long it’s been a part of my life, but for how much its taught me about myself. To find community, to learn through what we consume, only to use that to make something hopefully better. To embrace the freedoms of the world and take them no matter whose hands they’re in, and to use those freedoms to craft a more caring, fair, and loving future. One Piece feels like an extension of my own story. A way of seeing the world through different eyes. To take the opportunity to “set sail” for “everything this world has to offer.” It lies behind only a thousand chapters or a thousand episodes. So many have made the journey, and to those that have, we know how special it feels to be a part of a once in a lifetime story.↑

I’ve spent more time analyzing this story and the ways it reflects who I am than anything else. I’ve written a 136-page essay about its theming, watched its film over and over, listened to every intricacy of its soundtrack. This story is my everything. For a lost child who found it so many years ago it told him that he wasn’t alone, and for the kid who ran into it again 6 years later, it taught him everything he needed to know for the rollercoaster of a year that was awaiting him. This story is essentially my footing. Without this story, I think I’d have fallen a long time ago, and I’m not sure if I’d have had the strength to get up without what it taught me. It, along with life circumstances have created a lifelong dedication to suicide prevention and education within me. And for that, I don’t know if I could ever truly communicate what it means to me. A Silent Voice is the greatest story ever written, at least to me it is.↑

16. If the House told me to look inside, Undertale taught me it was time to come back out. I think I was ready now. I hadn’t seen every room, but standing in the next anteroom, I was able to peek into the surrounding corridors and feel like I had just the smallest understanding of how my brain was going to operate. Through all its stupid puns, its lighthearted humor, inside Undertale and its sequel Deltarune, there’s a real heart to be found. A story about finding salvation in the people around us. About learning to be and love oneself, not through solely deep introspection as I’d been doing, but by taking the chance to talk to another mind and find one’s own inside of it. That with enough determination, as long as we don’t give up, we will find our happiness. There’s no formula, no textbook, just us. Sometimes, that’s all we need. Even if it feels purposeless, even if we feel worthless, if we let the light of those around us drive us forward, we will survive. I think that’s what’s most important. Undertale has a weird reputation among internet fans, and I don’t blame them, but to me, it’s so much more than a goofy game about choosing whether to fight or not. It’s a lesson on how to treat the people around us, and the bonds that make those people the most important thing in life. There’s a quote in Deltarune that says, "Susie's smile, which seemed to defeat everything, shined on in front of you, and for a moment, it was as if all the darkness had been blown away.” Having heard these words, I learned to find that within my beautiful friends that I call my reason for living today. Without my internal compass shaped by this game, I don’t know if I’d be able to stand here today. Without any of these I’m not sure where I’d be, but I’m happy to be here. Even for just right now. I think that’s enough.↑
